ABSTRACT

In semi-urban municipalities such as Perinthalmanna and Valanchery (Malappuram district, Kerala), finding critical medicines during healthcare emergencies often leads to severe distress. Patients and caregivers are routinely forced to visit multiple physical medical stores or make numerous frantic phone calls to inquire about stock availability. Traditional e-commerce pharmacies fail in emergency scenarios because standard home deliveries take several hours or days, making them unsuitable for acute medical needs.

To resolve these critical challenges, this project introduces MediFind, an open, high-performance, full-stack hyperlocal medicine availability finder and digital prescription platform. MediFind is engineered using the Next.js 14 App Router framework with React 18 and TypeScript, backed by a cloud-native PostgreSQL relational database on Supabase, and client-side geospatial distance computation utilizing the Haversine formula.

MediFind incorporates five primary architectural modules: Hyperlocal Medicine and Pharmacy Search with Full-Text Search (FTS) engine and GPS proximity sorting; Online Medicine Stock Reservation; Digital Prescription Upload and Cloud Storage; Store Owner Inventory CRUD and Verification Suite; and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) with Row Level Security (RLS).

Keywords:

Medicine Availability, Digital Prescriptions, Hyperlocal Healthcare, Next.js 14, Supabase, PostgreSQL, Haversine Formula, Agile Scrum, Perinthalmanna, Valanchery.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

1.1 Overview

In modern healthcare systems, prompt access to prescribed medication is directly correlated with patient recovery and emergency stabilization. While major metropolitan cities benefit from commercial 10-minute medicine delivery apps, tier-2 and tier-3 towns such as Perinthalmanna and Valanchery in Malappuram district, Kerala, face an acute logistical bottleneck: retail pharmacy inventories are isolated and offline. When a doctor issues a specialized prescription (such as anti-hypertensives, specialized pediatric syrups, nebulizer solutions, or surgical supplies), caregivers routinely experience severe stress driving from one medical shop to another across town just to locate who has the item in stock.

MediFind addresses this critical societal challenge by creating an open, community-oriented digital directory for medicine stock. By combining client-side GPS proximity calculation, real-time inventory management for local retail pharmacists, and secure digital prescription uploads, MediFind bridges the communication gap between patients and pharmacies within minutes.

1.2 Problem Statement

The current physical-dependent model of finding medicines suffers from four principal deficiencies:

  1. Time-Sensitive Search Inefficiency: Visiting multiple shops in person or making blind phone calls wastes critical time during medical emergencies.
  2. National E-Commerce Delay: Commercial apps (Tata 1mg, Apollo 24/7, PharmEasy) fulfill orders from distant fulfillment warehouses requiring 24 to 48 hours for dispatch.
  3. Prescription Rejection at Counter: Patients carrying physical paper prescriptions frequently encounter prescription validation issues only after standing in line.
  4. Lack of Digital Infrastructure for Local Chemists: Local chemist shops lack accessible, low-barrier web portals to announce current stock to their immediate community.

1.3 Proposed System and Objectives

The MediFind platform provides a direct, accessible, and high-performance solution. The core objectives are:

  • Real-Time Medicine Search: Search across brand names (e.g., Dolo 650, Augmentin) and generic names (e.g., Paracetamol, Amoxicillin).
  • Proximity-Based Geo-Sorting: Automatically compute distances using the Haversine spherical formula and sort closest stores first.
  • Municipal Town Snapping: Allow instant snapping to key Malappuram commercial centers: Perinthalmanna, Valanchery, Kuttippuram, Kottakkal, Malappuram, and Tirur.
  • Digital Prescription Upload: Enable users to attach prescription scans directly to reservation requests.
  • Chemist Verification Suite: Enable pharmacists to view high-resolution uploaded prescriptions before marking items ready for counter pickup.
  • Trigger-Based Inventory Control: Automatically decrement on-shelf stock through database triggers when orders are marked as collected.

1.4 Scope and Feasibility Analysis

The project specifically targets the healthcare hubs of Perinthalmanna (known as the hospital city of Malappuram) and Valanchery, together with surrounding municipal networks in Kerala. The software architecture is containerized and cloud-hosted, allowing straightforward regional expansion.

Technical Feasibility

The platform is built on modern, open-source, production-ready web frameworks: Next.js 14, React 18, TypeScript, Supabase Cloud PostgreSQL 15, and Tailwind CSS. All components run efficiently across browsers without specialized client installations.

Economic and Operational Feasibility

Operating on serverless tiers, the platform requires no upfront capital expenditures or subscription licensing. Pharmacists can manage their inventory from any basic smartphone or desktop browser.

4.5 Automated Database Triggers

Trigger 1: sync_stock_status

Automatically updates the stock status enum (in_stock / low_stock / out_of_stock) based on quantity changes before every INSERT or UPDATE on pharmacy_stock.

  • quantity <= 0 → out_of_stock
  • quantity <= 10 → low_stock
  • quantity > 10 → in_stock

Trigger 2: decrement_stock_on_collection

Fires AFTER UPDATE on reservations. When status transitions to 'collected' from any other status, it decrements the corresponding pharmacy_stock quantity by the reservation quantity using GREATEST(0, quantity - new.quantity) to prevent negative stock.

Trigger 3: handle_new_user

Fires AFTER INSERT on auth.users (Supabase internal). Automatically creates a corresponding row in the profiles table with the user's email, full_name, and role extracted from raw_user_meta_data.

7.1 Haversine Geolocation Distance Calculation

The core geospatial engine implements the Haversine formula to compute the great-circle distance between two GPS coordinate pairs. This approach avoids expensive Google Maps API calls while maintaining sufficient accuracy for local proximity sorting.

export function calculateDistance(
  lat1: number, lon1: number,
  lat2: number, lon2: number
): number {
  const R = 6371; // Earth's mean radius in kilometers
  const dLat = (lat2 - lat1) * (Math.PI / 180);
  const dLon = (lon2 - lon1) * (Math.PI / 180);

  const a =
    Math.sin(dLat / 2) * Math.sin(dLat / 2) +
    Math.cos(lat1 * (Math.PI / 180)) *
    Math.cos(lat2 * (Math.PI / 180)) *
    Math.sin(dLon / 2) * Math.sin(dLon / 2);

  const c = 2 * Math.atan2(Math.sqrt(a), Math.sqrt(1 - a));
  return parseFloat((R * c).toFixed(1)); // Rounded distance in km
}

7.2 Automated Stock Decrement Trigger

When a pharmacy marks a reservation as 'collected', a PostgreSQL trigger automatically decrements the corresponding inventory without any additional API call from the frontend.

CREATE OR REPLACE FUNCTION public.decrement_stock_on_collection()
RETURNS trigger AS $$
BEGIN
  IF new.status = 'collected' AND old.status != 'collected' THEN
    UPDATE public.pharmacy_stock
    SET quantity = GREATEST(0, quantity - new.quantity)
    WHERE id = new.pharmacy_stock_id;
  END IF;
  RETURN new;
END;
$$ LANGUAGE plpgsql SECURITY DEFINER;

CREATE TRIGGER on_reservation_collected
  AFTER UPDATE ON public.reservations
  FOR EACH ROW
  EXECUTE FUNCTION public.decrement_stock_on_collection();

CHAPTER 10: CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE

10.1 Conclusion

The MediFind mini project successfully delivers a comprehensive, full-stack medicine availability and digital prescription platform tailored to the municipal healthcare needs of Perinthalmanna and Valanchery. Developed using Next.js 14, Supabase PostgreSQL, and client-side Haversine geospatial calculations, the platform bridges the critical divide between local retail pharmacies and urgent patient needs. The system eliminates emergency search distress, protects regulatory integrity through digital prescription verification, and automates retail inventory management through database triggers.

The project was executed strictly in accordance with KTU 20MCA245 syllabus requirements, adhering to Agile Scrum practices across five sprints, maintaining a detailed Scrum Book, and implementing automated deployment pipelines. All five primary objectives were achieved and validated through a comprehensive 10-case test suite with a 100% pass rate.

Key Contributions:

  • Developed a first-of-its-kind hyperlocal medicine search platform for Malappuram district, Kerala
  • Implemented the Haversine spherical formula in TypeScript for zero-cost GPS proximity sorting
  • Built a complete digital prescription lifecycle — patient upload, cloud storage, pharmacist verification, and counter fulfillment
  • Designed PostgreSQL trigger-based automated stock control eliminating manual inventory accounting errors
  • Deployed a production-grade, serverless application at zero infrastructure cost using Vercel and Supabase free tiers

10.2 Current Limitations

  • No home delivery or courier integration — counter-pickup only
  • No online payment gateway (Razorpay / UPI) integration
  • Admin Dashboard UI not yet implemented (backend RLS policies ready)
  • Manual pharmacy onboarding — no automated licensing verification
  • No push notifications — patients must manually check dashboard for status updates
  • Currently scoped to Malappuram district — not yet scaled nationally

10.3 Future Enhancements
